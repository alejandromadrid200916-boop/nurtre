Fiber

Why fiber matters for PCOS

Fiber is the part of plant foods your body can’t fully digest. With insulin resistance at the center of PCOS, it’s one of the most useful things to look for on your plate.

  • 🐢

    It slows things down

    Fiber slows digestion, so the sugar from a meal enters your blood more gradually and you stay full longer.

    Established guidance
  • 📉

    Low intake is linked to insulin resistance

    Studies have found that people with PCOS tend to eat less fiber, and lower intake has been linked with higher insulin resistance.

    Association
  • ❤️

    Soluble fiber helps cholesterol

    The soluble fiber in oats, barley, and beans can lower LDL cholesterol, which matters because PCOS raises long-term heart risk.

    Established guidance
  • 🦠

    It feeds your gut bacteria

    Gut bacteria break down fiber. Researchers are studying whether this affects hormones and inflammation in PCOS.

    Emerging hypothesis

Where to find the most fiber

Grams of fiber in one typical serving, from the food guide above. Beans and lentils fill most of the top spots.

    Easy ways to add more

    • 🫘Add beans or lentils to soups, salads, and sauces
    • 🍎Eat fruit whole, with the skin, instead of as juice
    • 🌾Swap one refined grain for a whole grain
    • 🌱Sprinkle chia, flax, or nuts on yogurt or oats

    Go gradually and drink water. Adding a lot of fiber at once can cause bloating, so give your body time to adjust. Established guidance

    Whole vs. processed

    Same food, different forms

    Processing isn’t all bad. Frozen vegetables and canned beans are great choices. The foods to keep occasional are the highly processed ones, which often lose fiber and gain added sugar, salt, or refined fats.

    Established guidance
