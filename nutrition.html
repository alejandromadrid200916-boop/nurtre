The heart of PCOS care

Nutrition is your most powerful tool for managing PCOS.

There's no cure for PCOS, but what you eat every day is one of the most effective ways to manage it. Healthy eating, together with regular activity, is the first-line approach recommended for everyone with PCOS, whether or not you also take medication.

Why nutrition comes first

Food can help break the insulin cycle

For many people with PCOS, insulin resistance sets off a chain reaction through the body's hormones. Everyday eating habits act on the very first link in that chain: steadier blood sugar means the body needs less insulin, which can mean fewer androgens and fewer symptoms over time.

Research finding

Consistency is what makes it work. These changes build over weeks and months, so lasting habits matter more than quick fixes.

What it can change

One change, benefits across your whole body

Because PCOS is a metabolic condition, improving how you eat reaches far beyond the scale.

  • Where it all starts

    Insulin & blood sugar

    Improves how your body handles insulin and blood sugar, a central driver of many PCOS symptoms.

    Established guidance

  • Cycles & ovulation

    Can support more regular periods and ovulation, especially with modest weight loss for those carrying extra weight.

    Established guidance

  • Skin & hair

    Lower insulin and androgen levels may ease acne and excess hair growth over time.

    Research finding

  • Weight

    Supports sustainable weight management. For those with excess weight, losing 5–10% can bring meaningful improvements.

    Established guidance

  • Long-term health

    Helps lower risk factors for type 2 diabetes and heart disease, which are more common with PCOS.

    Established guidance

How to eat for PCOS

Two ideas to build everything on

You don't need a strict plan. Keep coming back to these two ideas, and let the details follow.

  • Small steps, every day

    You don't have to change everything from one day to the next. One small change, repeated, becomes a habit, and habits are what add up over time.

    • Add one vegetable to a meal today
    • Make one swap this week
    • Keep what works, then build on it

    Consistency matters more than perfection.

  • Whole foods first

    Build most of your meals from whole and minimally processed foods: foods that look close to how they came from nature.

    • 🥬Leafy greens
    • 🥕Vegetables
    • 🍎Whole fruit
    • 🌾Whole grains
    • 🫘Beans
    • 🍗Lean proteins
    • 🐟Fish
    • 🥜Nuts & seeds
    Established guidance

Habits that help along the way

  • Keep meals regular

    Aim for regular meals and a consistent eating pattern through the week, instead of switching between restrictive diets.

  • Pair your carbs

    Pair carbohydrates with protein, fiber, or healthy fats to slow digestion and help manage blood sugar.

    Research finding

  • Skip the extremes

    No cutting out whole food groups or chasing trends. Weight loss can help some people with PCOS, but it should come from sustainable habits, not extreme restriction.

    Established guidance

  • Make it yours

    There's no single best diet for PCOS. Find an eating pattern that fits your lifestyle, preferences, and goals.

    Established guidance
