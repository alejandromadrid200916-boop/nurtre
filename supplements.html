Supplements

A supplement is just a supplement. A good diet can't be replaced.

Some supplements are being studied for PCOS, and some people need certain vitamins or minerals. They can add to healthy eating, but none of them replace it, and more isn't always better. Below, the most-researched PCOS supplements come first.

Trying to conceive or doing IVF?

What to know before adding fertility supplements

Supplements are often marketed to improve egg quality or IVF success. Here's what the evidence actually supports.

Better lab numbers don't always mean a better chance of having a baby.

A supplement might improve egg counts or embryo scores without increasing the chance of a live birth, and that's the outcome that matters most.

Research finding
  • 🥬

    Folate is standard care

    Folate before pregnancy is recommended for everyone trying to conceive. It's basic prenatal care, not a special fertility booster.

    Established guidance
  • 🩸

    Fix deficiencies first

    Testing and correcting low levels of nutrients like vitamin D, iron, or B12 is different from taking extra to try to boost fertility.

    Established guidance
  • 🌸

    Inositol is still experimental for fertility

    The 2023 PCOS guideline says it may be considered, but its effects on ovulation, pregnancy, and live birth are uncertain.

    Established guidance
  • ⏳

    Don't delay treatment

    Don't postpone recommended fertility treatment to finish a supplement plan, especially if age or egg reserve is a concern.

    Established guidance
  • 🔁

    Watch for doubling up

    Prenatal vitamins and fertility blends often overlap in folate, vitamin D, iron, iodine, selenium, zinc, and vitamin A.

    Established guidance
  • 🗒️

    Bring your full list and a stop date

    Include powders, teas, and imported products, and ask your care team when to stop each one, since safety after conception is often less certain.

    Established guidance

    Safety

    More isn't always better

    Supplements are powerful, and "natural" doesn't mean risk-free. Keep these in mind.

    • 🥕

      Some vitamins build up

      Vitamins A, D, E, and K are stored in the body, so high doses over time can become harmful.

      Established guidance
    • 🧂

      Minerals have limits

      Too much zinc, selenium, iodine, or iron can cause problems. Take what you need, not more.

      Established guidance
    • 🤰

      Pregnancy changes things

      Berberine isn't safe during pregnancy, and high doses of vitamin A should be avoided. Folate needs go up.

      Established guidance
    • 💬

      Share everything you take

      Supplements can interact with medicines, like vitamin K with blood thinners or berberine with diabetes medicines. Include teas and powders too.

      Established guidance
    • 🍃

      Herbs aren't automatically safe

      Concentrated herbal extracts can interact with medicines, and many haven't been well studied in pregnancy.

      Established guidance
    • 🏷️

      Choose tested products

      Supplements aren't regulated as strictly as medicines, and labels don't always match what's inside. Look for independent testing, such as a USP or NSF seal.

      Established guidance
